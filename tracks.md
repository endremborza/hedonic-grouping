Because your paper sits at the intersection of computer science, algorithms, and economics, you have two primary tracks you can pursue.

**Track 1: Algorithmic Game Theory & Computer Science (Recommended)**
These venues are highly receptive to complex algorithms solving NP-hard coalition formation games.
1.  **EC (ACM Conference on Economics and Computation):** The premier conference for the intersection of computer science and economics. A unifying algorithmic framework for Hedonic Games would be highly relevant here.
2.  **SAGT (International Symposium on Algorithmic Game Theory):** An excellent, highly respected conference specifically focused on the computational aspects of game theory, matching, and mechanism design.
3.  **WINE (Conference on Web and Internet Economics):** Despite the name, this conference heavily features matching theory, coalition formation, and algorithm design.
4.  **AAMAS (International Conference on Autonomous Agents and Multiagent Systems):** Hedonic games and coalition formation are major tracks at AAMAS, as agents forming teams is a core multi-agent systems problem.

**Track 2: Economics and Mechanism Design**
If you choose to focus heavily on the *mechanism* and the theoretical guarantees rather than the computational Big-O complexity, consider these journals.
1.  **Games and Economic Behavior:** The top journal for game theory. They publish foundational theoretical work on matching and stability.
2.  **Journal of Mechanism and Institution Design:** A great open-access journal focused specifically on the rules and algorithms governing matching markets and economic interactions. 
3.  **Theoretical Computer Science (Track C - Theory of Algorithms):** If your Big-O complexity analysis and halting proofs are mathematically rigorous, this is a strong journal target.
